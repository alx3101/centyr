## 0. The Core Correction

The previous version anchored the entire perceived value on **one wedge** — the Amazon-square-vs-Zalando-portrait rejection. That made Centyr look like a niche "marketplace format converter." The real, defensible value is broader and deeper: **Centyr turns a catalog into a reproducible standard that stays uniform over time.** Perception must lead with that. The multichannel format conflict is a *supporting* pain named for one subset, not the headline for everyone.

Three pillars, in perceived-value order:
1. **Catalog uniformity over time** (the moat — new products auto-match, one-click reprocess). Primary.
2. **Multichannel compliance** (one upload → every platform's format). Subset hook.
3. **Done-for-you compliance** (we track the specs so you don't). Peace-of-mind.

## 1. Current Perception Issues

- **Over-narrowed category**: framing as "EU marketplace image compliance engine" hides the primary value (a persistent catalog standard) and shrinks the market to multichannel sellers. The real category — "catalog standardization that holds over time" — is one Centyr owns and that every catalog operator needs, multichannel or not.
- **Uniformity-over-time was invisible**: the single most defensible thing Centyr does — *new products auto-match the existing catalog; change the standard and reprocess everything in one click* — barely appeared. That is the moat; it must be the centerpiece.
- **Feature-first descriptions**: "BiRefNet," "auto-centering," "presets" describe inputs. Buyers pay for the outcome: a catalog that matches and stays matched.
- **Fabricated-proof risk**: prior bonus valuations and ROI multiples (100:1, 122:1) read like invented results. At pre-revenue with zero customers, perceived value must come from the product working on the prospect's *own* catalog — not numbers we can't stand behind.
- **Agencies/studios under-weighted**: the high-ARPA multiplier (many client catalogs, one standard each, one-click reprocess) was a footnote. It should be a named value driver.
- **Wrong price anchors**: previous docs referenced €49–€99 tiers that don't exist. Real Stripe pricing is Free / €12.99 / €39.99 / €129. Perceived value must be justified against the *real* price.

## 3. Packaging Upgrade — Named Three-Pillar System

**The Centyr Catalog Standard**

**Pillar 1 — The Standard Engine (the moat; lead here)**
- Aligns, centers, sizes, removes background (BiRefNet — strong on glass, jewelry, reflective, transparent), and normalizes exposure across your whole catalog to one standard.
- **Auto-Match**: every new product you add inherits the existing standard automatically — the catalog stays uniform as it grows.
- **One-Click Reprocess**: change your margin, size, format, or background and re-apply it to your entire catalog in one click — no redoing anything by hand.

**Pillar 2 — One Source, Every Channel (the multichannel subset)**
- One standardized catalog exports to Amazon (1:1 square), Zalando (2:3 portrait), eBay, Etsy, and your own store — each compliant, from the same source. Resolves the square-vs-portrait conflict for multichannel sellers without a second pass.

**Pillar 3 — Done-for-You Compliance (peace of mind)**
- Centyr keeps up with each marketplace's exact image spec so you never track, learn, or maintain them. "We stay compliant with every marketplace's spec so you don't have to think about it."

## 6. Anchoring (honest, against real pricing)

**Primary anchor (uniformity over time):**
Aligning ~200 products into a matching grid by hand takes ~20 hours (≈ €300 of work) — and you do it again every season and every time you add products. Fiverr redoes it from scratch each cycle and never keeps your standard. Centyr does it in ~10 minutes, then **holds the standard**: new products auto-match, and one click re-applies changes to the whole catalog. Pro is €39.99/month.

**Secondary anchor (reproducibility):**
The value isn't one clean-up — it's that you never lose the standard. A freelancer's result dies when the job ends; Centyr's standard is yours and re-applies on demand. That's the difference between paying for labor again and again versus owning a system.

**Multichannel anchor (subset only):**
If you sell on both Amazon (square) and Zalando (portrait), one upload gives you both — no second manual pass per platform, no reshoot.

**Do not use:** invented ROI multiples, studio invoices of €6,000, or "100:1 in month one." No customers exist to substantiate them. The honest, verifiable numbers are: ~200 products in ~10 min vs ~20 hours (≈ €300), and a single product in ~21 seconds.
