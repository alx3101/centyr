# Centyr — Positioning Angles
# Offer: The Centyr Catalog Standard

---

## Base Offer

- **Who**: E-commerce sellers, brands, artisans, and agencies/studios with real, growing catalogs (dozens–hundreds of SKUs) that update seasonally and must stay visually uniform over time — on Amazon/eBay/Etsy/Zalando and/or their own store. Multichannel sellers are a high-value subset; agencies/studios are the high-ARPA multiplier.
- **What**: Turn a catalog into a reproducible standard — align, center, size, remove background, normalize exposure so every product matches — that holds over time (new products auto-match; one-click reprocess when the standard changes) and outputs compliant for every channel from one upload.
- **Reframe rule**: Lead with **uniformity over time** (the moat). The Amazon-vs-Zalando format conflict is **one angle for the multichannel subset — not the spine of every message.**

---

## The Angle Set

Angles are grouped by the three pillars, moat first.

### PILLAR 1 — Catalog uniformity over time (primary)

#### Angle A: Uniformity-over-time (LEAD ANGLE)
The core promise: every product photo matches, and stays matched as the catalog grows.
- **V1**: "Your catalog looks like it was shot by five different people. Centyr aligns, centers, sizes, and evens out every product photo so they all match — and stay matched as you add more."
- **V2**: "Set your product-photo standard once. Every product in your catalog matches it — automatically."

#### Angle B: Auto-match-new-products
The moat, made concrete: growth stops breaking uniformity.
- **V1**: "Add a new product three months from now and it auto-matches the 500 already in your catalog. No re-aligning to keep up."
- **V2**: "Your catalog grows. Your standard doesn't move. Every new SKU inherits it the moment you upload."

#### Angle C: Reprocess-in-1-click
Change is cheap; the standard is re-applicable.
- **V1**: "Change your margin, size, or background and re-apply it to your entire catalog in one click. Fiverr and Canva would start over from zero."
- **V2**: "Rebrand your whole catalog's look in one click — not one product at a time."

#### Angle D: Cost-vs-studio/Fiverr (reproducibility economics)
Why owning a standard beats renting labor.
- **V1**: "Aligning ~200 products by hand takes ~20 hours (about €300 of work) — and you redo it every season. Centyr does it in ~10 minutes and keeps the standard so you never redo it."
- **V2**: "A freelancer's result dies when the job ends. Centyr's standard is yours — and re-applies on demand, forever. Pro is €39.99/month."

### PILLAR 2 — Multichannel compliance (one angle for the subset)

#### Angle E: Multichannel compliance
One standardized catalog, every platform's format — from one upload.
- **V1**: "One upload becomes Amazon-square, Zalando-portrait, eBay, Etsy, and your own store — each compliant, from the same standardized catalog."
- **V2**: "Sell everywhere without reformatting for everywhere. One source, every channel."

#### Angle E-Zalando: The format-conflict pain (named, but not the spine)
Use for the multichannel subset who feel the square-vs-portrait conflict acutely.
- **V1**: "Amazon wants square. Zalando wants portrait. Instead of two manual passes, upload once — Centyr outputs both from the same source."
- **V2**: "If Zalando rejected your images for being square, you don't need a reshoot. You need one catalog that outputs every marketplace's format."
- **Note**: This is one hook among several. Do not build the whole offer on it.

### PILLAR 3 — Done-for-you compliance (peace of mind)

#### Angle F: Done-for-you-compliance
We track the changing specs so the seller doesn't.
- **V1**: "Every marketplace publishes its own image spec, and they change. Centyr keeps up with all of them so you never have to learn, track, or maintain a single one."
- **V2**: "We stay compliant with every marketplace's spec so you don't have to think about it."

### CROSS-CUTTING

#### Angle G: Agency/studio multiplier (high-ARPA)
Many client catalogs, each with its own persistent standard.
- **V1**: "Managing catalogs for ten clients? Give each one its own standard, batch-process it, and reprocess it in one click when the client changes their mind — without hiring."
- **V2**: "Turn per-image retouching labor into per-catalog standards you can re-run on demand. One operator, many catalogs, one throughput multiplier."

#### Angle H: Effort/identity
- **V1**: "No Canva grid. No Photoshop. No Fiverr re-briefing. No spec sheets. Upload your catalog, download a matching one."
- **V2**: "Run your catalog like a real brand — one consistent standard, maintained automatically — instead of improvising every season."

## Top 3 Angles

**1. Angle A — Uniformity-over-time (LEAD).**
Why it works: It names the pain every catalog operator feels regardless of channel — the catalog doesn't match — and it's the broadest, most honest promise. It opens the whole market (sellers, brands, artisans, agencies), not just multichannel sellers. It sets up the moat (B and C) as the reason to believe.
Best deployment: The first line of almost every touchpoint; cold outreach paired with the prospect's own before/after.

**2. Angle B — Auto-match-new-products (the moat).**
Why it works: This is what nothing else does. Fiverr and Canva clean up a catalog once; only Centyr keeps it uniform as it grows. It converts "nice one-time tool" into "system I can't stop using," which is the retention and differentiation story in one line.
Best deployment: Right after Angle A, as the "and it stays that way" payoff; retention and upgrade messaging.

**3. Angle D — Cost-vs-studio/Fiverr (reproducibility economics).**
Why it works: It quantifies the value honestly (~20 hours ≈ €300 per cycle, repeated every season) and frames the real contrast — owning a reproducible standard vs. renting labor that has to be re-briefed every time. No invented ROI multiples; the numbers are verifiable.
Best deployment: Pricing page, ROI section, and any message where the buyer is comparing Centyr to a freelancer or studio.

## Recommended Use

- **Cold outreach (highest signal)**: Angle A + the prospect's own before/after grid. This is the only honest proof at pre-revenue stage. Add Angle D for the ROI case.
- **Landing page**: Angle A as headline, Angle B + C as the "stays matched / one-click reprocess" proof of moat, Angle E as a section for multichannel sellers, Angle F for peace-of-mind. (Landing copy itself is handled in a separate pass — this is the angle guidance.)
- **Multichannel subset (Amazon+Zalando etc.)**: Angle E / E-Zalando as the entry hook, then pivot to Angle A/B so the value doesn't collapse back to "format converter."
- **Agencies / studios (high-ARPA)**: Angle G + C — persistent per-client standards and one-click reprocess. This is the Business-tier driver.
- **Organic / community content**: Angle H (identity/effort) and Angle B (auto-match) reframe the belief that catalog uniformity is a recurring manual chore.
- **Honesty guardrail**: never imply existing customers, testimonials, or results. Proof = the product works + the prospect's own before/after.
